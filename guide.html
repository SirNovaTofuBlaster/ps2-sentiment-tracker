1. What the dashboard shows

A robot on GitHub reads gaming news sites, Reddit, forums, 4chan's game boards, YouTube channels and podcasts all day long, each on its own clock: 4chan every 15 minutes, news sites and forums every 30, Reddit and YouTube every hour, podcasts every 6 hours. For every headline, thread, video and episode it checks whether a PlayStation 2 game is mentioned, whether it sounds like remaster or remake news, and how positive the wording is. Every game it recognises gets links out to shops and price guides, and eBay's asking prices beside its name. The dashboard shows the result:

  • Four pages, chosen with the tabs under the banner. Tracker has the numbers, the games named in the last 24 hours, the Demand Index, the table and the sources. Rarest lists the hundred rarest PAL games, the hundred rarest US games and the rarest official PS2 consoles, with their eBay prices (see The rarest games). Most mentioned ranks every PS2 game the feed names. eBay prices opens with the PS2 Price Index and then lists what used copies of every tracked game are listed for. On a screen wider than a phone, the number on a tab is how many games that page holds. Your browser's Back button returns to the page you were on, and adding #rare, #mentions or #prices to the address opens a page directly.
  • Named in the last 24 hours, first on the Tracker: the PS2 games named by anything published in the last day, and nothing else. Most of what the robot reads names no PS2 game at all, so this list is where to look first. It is ordered by price: first the games with an eBay UK median, the most expensive at the top down to the cheapest; then the games with only a US price, the same way (dollars and pounds are never compared); then the games with no price at all. Games at the same price, and the games without one, keep the usual order: the most headlines first, so one found only in the text under a headline (marked in the text only; those are wrong more often) comes later, then the most mentions, then the most recent. Each line says how many items named the game and which sources, with its prices. Click a game's name to jump to the table showing exactly the items that name it, from the whole two weeks and not only the last day; Back to every game above the table, or any change to the search box or the menus, returns the table to normal. The line under the heading says how many items were published in the last day and how many of them named a game. "Published" is the date the source gave the item, so when a source is first added, its older items go straight to the table. (A source that gives no dates has its items dated when the robot first sees them.) The list is as good as the name matching: a series still going today is counted for its PS2 game, and an ordinary phrase is sometimes taken for a title.
  • eBay median: under a game's name, wherever one appears (the last 24 hours, Demand Index, Most Mentioned Games and the table), you see the middle asking price of used copies on eBay US and eBay UK. Click a figure to search that eBay site for the game; hover over it for how many copies it covers and when it was checked. Once a game has been priced for a week, a bracket beside the median says how it moved since a week before: £73.49 (£2.30 ▲ 3.2%) means up £2.30, or 3.2%, and ▼ means down; (no change) means the same. Hover over the bracket for the week-old figure and its date. A game's first bracket appears a week after its first price. The earliest prices are from the night of 6 October 2026, so the first brackets appear late on 13 October (UK time), and for most games during the 14th, as each comes up for its next check. When a game's search words have just been changed, its bracket compares two different searches for a week, so a big jump then says more about the search than about the market. A game with no such line is one that isn't priced: see eBay asking prices.
  • The four numbers at the top: how many items the feed holds, how many look like remaster news, how many sources are switched on, and the overall mood from 0 to 100. The first one is not a running total: the robot keeps the last two weeks and drops anything older, so the label says how far back the items go ("Items · 9 days") and the number levels off once two weeks are in. A number that stays the same does not mean the robot has stopped; Last sync in the banner is what tells you when it last ran.
  • Most Mentioned Games, on the Most mentioned tab: every PS2 game the feed names, the most mentioned first, with each one's mention count and share of all game mentions. An item that names several games counts as a mention of each. It recalculates whenever the feed loads or you switch a source on or off.
  • Lists that fold away: the last 24 hours shows its first ten games, the Demand Index its first five, and Most Mentioned Games and each list of the rarest games their first 25. Press Show … more under a list to open the rest (up to 25 games for the Demand Index, every game for Most Mentioned and the rarest games), and Show fewer to fold it again; that button follows you down the list. A list stays the way you left it until you reload the page.
  • Striped rows: every second row of those lists and of the table sits on a slightly lighter shade, so your eye can follow one row across.
  • The table: it opens on the items that name a PS2 game, and the line just above it says how many those are and out of how many. Everything else that was collected is still there: choose Everything in the first menu to see it, or Remaster News Only for remaster news. The search box looks at headlines, sources and every game an item names, and the second menu narrows the table to one kind of source. When an item names more than one game, its row shows the first with its prices (or the game you clicked in the list at the top) and lists the others under it ("also names …"); a game the search box found is listed before the rest.
  • On a phone: the table becomes a list of cards, 25 to a page, and each game's price links sit on one line that you swipe sideways.

Times are shown in your own time zone. The robot works in UTC, so "Last sync" and the times in the table are converted to wherever you are. Hover over a time to see the original.

About the mood score. It reads the wording of a headline, not what the headline is about. Words like "masterpiece" or "underrated" push it up, "broken" or "disappointing" push it down, and "not a masterpiece" counts as criticism rather than praise. Announcements are deliberately neutral: a remaster being announced is news, not good news, so words like "remaster" and "announced" no longer raise the score. It is a rough guide to tone and nothing more.

About the 4chan rows. From 4chan the robot takes one thing only: which PS2 games a thread names. Nothing a poster wrote is copied, kept or shown. The line you read in the table, such as "Thread on /vr/ naming Silent Hill 2", is written by the robot from the board and the game names. These rows have a dash where the mood score would be, they are never flagged as remaster news, and they never count towards the overall mood. They do count in Most Mentioned Games. Three things to know:

  • Only the post that starts a thread is read, not the replies under it.
  • The rules for a name are stricter than for a headline, because posts are chat. The whole name has to be there, spelt the way the PS2 library spells it, and written as a name: with its number ("silent hill 2") or with capital letters ("Top Gun", not "top gun"). A name followed by another game's number does not count ("Max Payne 2" is not Max Payne), and a single everyday word such as "Black" does not count as the first word of a sentence. Some real mentions are missed that way; far fewer wrong ones get in.
  • The Thread button opens the thread on 4chan, where you will see everything posted in it, unfiltered. Threads there are deleted after a few hours or days, so an older row's button leads to a page that no longer exists. The row stays for two weeks like any other.

About the remaster flag. Clear wording — remaster, remake, reboot, director's cut — flags on its own. Everyday words like "collection", "ports" or "returns" only flag when a PS2 game was also recognised in the same headline, because "my PS2 collection" isn't news.

2. The price links

Every recognised game carries a row of small yellow buttons, split into PAL (Europe) and US. They are searches, not prices: clicking one opens that shop or price guide with the game already searched for. The only actual figures on the dashboard are the eBay medians shown just above these buttons.

  • Price Charting tracks what copies have actually sold for, which is the closest thing to a market value. There is a separate PAL and US price for most games, and they can differ a lot.
  • eBay shows what sellers are asking today, not what anything sold for. Asking prices usually sit well above real ones, and a single graded or sealed copy can make a game look far more expensive than it is.
  • CeX is one shop's own price in the UK. It's a real "what would this cost me this afternoon" figure, but it only covers games CeX still trades and it moves with their stock rather than with the market.

Because these are searches, you may land on the wrong edition or a game with a similar name — always check the result matches the game and the region before trusting a number.

3. The Demand Index

This section only appears once the daily job has run; if you don't see it, nothing is wrong. It asks a different question from Most Mentioned Games: not "what is being talked about here" but "what are people looking up".

It counts how often each game's Wikipedia article was read over the last four weeks and blends that with how often the game is mentioned in this feed. The arrow and percentage are the interesting part: they compare the last seven days against the seven before, so a game climbing sharply shows up even if a famous game has more readers overall.

It updates once a day, because Wikipedia only publishes its reading figures daily. A game that appears with no percentage simply hasn't enough history yet.

4. eBay asking prices

Once an hour, a second job asks eBay what used copies of each game are listed for, on eBay US and eBay UK separately. A US copy and a UK copy are different products, so the two are never mixed.

The figures show in two places. On the Tracker and Most mentioned pages, every game that is priced has an eBay median line under its name: the middle asking price on each site (the middle one, so a single silly listing can't drag it about). The eBay prices tab lists every tracked game in full: the median (with its change since a week before, once there is one), the cheapest, how many copies are listed and how long ago eBay was asked, with a box to find a game by name (it finds Kingdom Hearts II whether you type "kingdom hearts 2" or "hearts ii"). Until the price job has run for the first time, that tab says so and no medians are shown anywhere; nothing is wrong.

The PS2 Price Index, at the top of the eBay prices tab, is one number per site that says which way PS2 prices are going, like a stock market index. It started at 100 on 6 October 2026. Each day it moves by the typical change in each game's median since the last day that game was priced (up to three days before), so a game being added or dropped never moves it, and an expensive game counts no more than a cheap one. Beside it: how it moved since the day before and over a week, each named by the day it is compared with, and a line of every day so far. A day counts once it is over (UTC), so the latest point is yesterday's. Thin figures (fewer than three copies listed) are not in it, nor is a median that more than doubled or halved, which nearly always means the search found something different; a smaller change to a game's search words can still nudge it a little. A day when too few games could be compared keeps the value it had, and its card says so. US and UK are separate; consoles are not in it. It shows up after the first price run that writes it.

These are what sellers are asking for copies still for sale, not what anything sold for: asking prices usually sit above real ones, and loose discs are counted together with complete copies.

  • Which games: every game the feed has ever mentioned, as long as it is in the PS2 library. Games with a one-word name that is also an ordinary word, such as Black, Cars or The Sims, are left out, because a search for the word finds every listing that happens to use it. Those rows keep their yellow buttons and simply have no median. The games on the Rarest page are priced too, whether the feed has named them or not.
  • One site only: when just US or just UK is shown under a name, eBay had no copy the robot could count on the other site.
  • Different spellings: the feed may call a game Persona 4 or Kingdom Hearts 2 where the price list says Shin Megami Tensei: Persona 4 or Kingdom Hearts II. They are treated as the same game.
  • Games with another name in Europe: some games were released in Europe under a different name, such as Fatal Frame as Project Zero, Siren as Forbidden Siren or Dark Cloud 2 as Dark Chronicle. eBay UK is searched under the European name, so their UK prices come from PAL copies. The list of these names is in ebay_watchlist.json on GitHub; tell the repository's owner about any that are missing.
  • surging: a game that is rarely mentioned was suddenly named in headlines by two or more sources within a day. It is checked on every run for the next two days. About half of these turn out to be the robot matching the wrong game, so read it as "worth a look".
  • staple: named on five or more of the last fourteen days, so one more mention is no news. quiet: not mentioned for two weeks. Staples and everything untagged are checked about every 6 hours, quiet games once a day. rarest: on the Rarest page and not named by the feed; checked once a day, and only on its own region's site (a PAL game on eBay UK, a US game on eBay US).
  • A + after the number of copies means eBay had more listings than it hands over at once (200), so the figures cover the first 200.
  • A dimmed price has gone longer than usual without a check: over 8 hours, or over 30 for a quiet game. GitHub sometimes runs the jobs late.
  • "in headlines" counts headlines that named the game in the last two weeks. Most Mentioned Games also counts mentions found in the text under a headline, so the two numbers can differ.
  • "none listed" means eBay was asked and no copy was counted. "search needs tuning" means eBay returned listings but none of them named the game, so there is no figure rather than a wrong one. "not checked yet" means its turn hasn't come.
  • The list on the eBay prices tab is ordered with surging games first, then by how often each is mentioned, and quiet games last.

Only numbers are kept: the current figures, and a history of how they change, in the data/prices folder on GitHub. To always track a game, give one its own search words, or stop one being priced, the repository's owner edits ebay_watchlist.json.

5. The rarest games

The Rarest tab has three lists: the hundred rarest PAL games (Europe and other PAL countries), the hundred rarest US games, and the rarest official Consoles. Press the coloured PAL (blue), US (red) or Consoles (teal) buttons at the top right to switch between them; the lit one is the list on screen. The PAL list is priced on eBay UK and the US list on eBay US.

  • How they are ranked: nobody counts how many copies of a game were made or still exist, so collectors go by price. Each list is ranked by guide, PriceCharting's market price for a complete copy (game, case and manual) in US dollars, as read on the date in the line under the heading. That is where cheap "shovelware" that was printed in tiny numbers, such as Sengoku Anthology or Gun Club, comes out above famous games.
  • The eBay price under each game is the median asking price for a used copy on that region's own site, checked once a day: eBay UK for the PAL list, eBay US for the US list. It works like the rest of the dashboard's eBay figures (see eBay asking prices): it is what sellers ask, not what anything sold for, and rare games often have few or no copies for sale, so "none listed" is common and a single copy can make the median jump. "not priced" means the game is never looked up, usually because its name is an everyday word (Obscure, Buccaneer) and an eBay search would count other games' listings.
  • US name / PAL name: the name the same game has in the other region, where it differs.
  • Sources: the links under each game are where its place on the list comes from (PriceCharting, and articles such as Racketboy's where they mention the game). Hover over a link for the figure it gives and its date.
  • Tags: thin data means the guide value rests on one or two sales; one source means only PriceCharting gives a figure; edition is a special edition or a set sold under its own name; India is a PAL game released for India rather than Europe, which hardly ever appears on eBay UK.
  • How sure the order is: the top of each list is backed by several sources. Lower down most places rest on PriceCharting alone, and below roughly $60 (PAL) or $90 (US) the order may have gaps. The lists were researched on 8 October 2026 and are not updated by themselves; the eBay prices are.

The consoles are official Sony hardware only: limited colours, special editions, Sony devices with a PS2 inside (the PSX recorders, the Bravia TV) and development units. Custom paint jobs, shells and modded consoles are never on the list. They are split by where they were sold: press US, Japan or PAL under the heading (Japan opens first, as most of them were Japanese). A console sold in several regions is in each of their lists. Each list is sorted by price, most expensive first: by the eBay median on the site that sells that region's copies (eBay UK for PAL, eBay US for the US and Japan), then by the other site's median; consoles with no copies listed come after the priced ones, by their guide value. How rare each one is shows as its tier:

  • tier 1: a known run of 5,000 or fewer, such as the five car-paint colours of 2001, about 666 of each per region. tier 2: never sold in shops, or sold through one shop or in one bundle. tier 3: a limited colour of one country, most of them Japan's. tier 4: limited across several countries, or made in larger numbers. Hover over a tier for its meaning.
  • never sold is a development or test unit; unconfirmed rests on one weak source.
  • Under each console: its model numbers, where and when it came out, how many were made when anyone has said, and the eBay median on both eBay US and eBay UK (or only the site of the one country it was sold in). These include sellers anywhere, because most of these consoles were only sold in Japan and are listed from there. A listing counts only when it names the model or its colour and is a working console; bundles with controllers or games count, but shells, skins, custom paint, mods and broken consoles never do.
  • Three are not priced: the one-off show unit, and two whose model numbers could not be confirmed. Hover over "not priced" for why.

The lists live in rare_games.json and rare_consoles.json on GitHub, with every source and the reasons for each place. To move, add or remove a game, the repository's owner edits that file; the price job picks the change up on its next run.

6. The Sources & Weights panel

Click Sources at the top of the dashboard. There is one tab per kind of source (News sites, Reddit, Forums, 4chan, YouTube, Podcasts) and a Weights & schedule tab.

  • Switch a source on or off with the checkbox at the start of its row. Its items disappear from the dashboard straight away. Nothing is deleted: switch it back on whenever you like.
  • Find a source with the search box, or sort the list, e.g. YouTube by subscribers or total views and podcasts by Apple ratings or chart position.
  • The numbers (subscribers, views, ratings) were measured on the date shown at the top of the panel. The Status column is live.
  • On a phone the tabs are on one line that swipes sideways, each source is a card, and the list scrolls inside its own box so the form for adding a source stays close by.
  • A changed list shows an Unsaved changes badge. Your changes only affect your screen until you save them. Discard undoes everything since the last save.

7. Add a source

Open the tab for the kind of source, paste the link into the box under the list and press Add source. The name is filled in for you where possible. Group only sorts the list, and Role sets its weight.

YouTube channel

Open the channel on YouTube and copy the address from the address bar, e.g. https://www.youtube.com/@IGN. Video links don't work. The robot looks the channel up on its next run; if it can't, the status says so (see below).

Podcast

Find the show on Apple Podcasts and paste its link, e.g. https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/retronauts/id672857593. The name and feed are filled in automatically. Spotify links can't be used because Spotify doesn't publish feeds.

News site

Paste the site's RSS feed link. Many sites have one at their address followed by /feed or /rss; otherwise look for an RSS icon. Give it a name.

Forum

Paste the RSS feed link of one board of the forum and give it a name. On many forums it is the board's address followed by index.rss. Be ready for it not to work: many big forums refuse visitors that come from GitHub's computers, which is where the robot runs. The status will say "Failing: HTTP 403" after the next run; switch the forum off again if it does.

4chan board

Type the board's short name (vr, /vr/) or paste its link. As with the other boards, only the names of the PS2 games its threads mention are kept (see About the 4chan rows). A board has no Weight box, because its rows have no mood score to weigh.

Subreddit

Type its name (ps2, r/ps2) or paste its link. Keep the suggested group: subreddits in one group are read together, because Reddit only allows about one request a minute.

Made a typo? A source you just added has a button until you save. Sources that are already saved can only be switched off, so nothing gets lost by accident.

8. Weights

A weight says how much a source's items count towards the overall mood figure. It doesn't change a single headline's own score, and it doesn't affect which games are recognised.

WEIGHTMEANING
1.5counts one and a half times: PS2-only sources such as r/ps2 and PS2 channels
1normal: news, official channels, Reddit, forums, retro channels
0.5counts half: collectors and resellers ("INSANE PS2 HAUL!!") and big entertainment channels
0still shown in the table, but doesn't count at all
  • Change a whole kind of source in the Weights & schedule tab, e.g. set Collectors & resellers to 0.2.
  • Change a single source by typing a number in its Weight box. Leave it empty to use its role's weight.
  • The dashboard updates immediately. Save to keep it.
  • Anonymous boards (4chan) is listed at 0, and whatever number is put there, 4chan rows never count: they have no mood score.

A PS2-only source does one other thing: the robot treats anything it publishes as being about the PS2, which helps it recognise games with short or ordinary names like Ico or Black that it would otherwise ignore.

The same tab has the fetch schedule: how often each kind of source is read. Choose from 15 or 30 minutes up to 24 hours for each kind. The robot keeps running and reads a kind as soon as its time comes round. Podcasts are read every 6 hours because their feeds are large. Reddit only allows about one request a minute, so the robot asks for three subreddits at a time, newest posts first, and a Reddit round takes about ten minutes. Run scraper now, or saving your sources, restarts the robot and reads everything at once.

9. Save your changes

The list of sources is a file called feeds.json on GitHub. Saving means putting the new version there, which needs a GitHub account that can edit the repository.

  1. Press Save changes. Your changes are copied and a box with the next steps appears.
  2. Click Open feeds.json on GitHub (sign in if GitHub asks).
  3. Click inside the file, press Ctrl+A to select everything, then Ctrl+V to paste. On a Mac: ⌘+A, ⌘+V.
  4. Click the green Commit changes... button, then Commit changes again.
  5. Back on the dashboard, click I've saved it to clear the badge.

The robot starts by itself and new results appear within a few minutes.

Select everything before pasting. Pasting into the middle of the file leaves a mixture of the old and new lists, which the robot refuses to read — it keeps the previous data until the file is valid again.

If GitHub first asks you to Fork this repository, your account can't edit it directly. That still works: click Fork this repository, paste as above, click Propose changes, then Create pull request. The owner gets a request, and your change applies once they accept it. You can also press Download on the dashboard and send the file to the owner.

Commits made on github.com use your account's email settings. To keep your email private, turn on Keep my email addresses private in GitHub email settings.

10. One-click saving with a token (optional)

A token is a password that lets this page save for you. With one, Save changes saves in one click and Run scraper now works. Which kind you need depends on who owns the repository:

The repository is yours

  1. Open new fine-grained token.
  2. Name it "PS2 dashboard" and pick an expiry (e.g. 90 days).
  3. Under Repository access choose Only select repositories and pick this repository.
  4. Under Permissions set Contents to Read and write (and Actions too, for Run scraper now).
  5. Press Generate token and copy it (it starts with github_pat_).

You're a collaborator on someone else's repository

  1. GitHub doesn't allow fine-grained tokens here, so open new classic token.
  2. Name it "PS2 dashboard" and pick an expiry (e.g. 90 days).
  3. Tick only public_repo. That is enough to save, but it covers all your public repositories, so keep the expiry short. Run scraper now would need the much broader repo box; you don't need it because saving starts the scraper anyway.
  4. Press Generate token and copy it (it starts with ghp_).

Then, on the dashboard: Sources & Weights → GitHub settings, paste the token, check that the repository is filled in (it is on the website) and press Save settings. Tick Remember on this device only on your own computer: it keeps the token in this browser for the whole github.io site.

The token works like a password: don't share it. This page keeps it in your browser and only sends it to GitHub. Forget token removes it from the browser. You can cancel a token at any time in GitHub's settings: fine-grained tokens or classic tokens. Saves made with a token use your GitHub no-reply email.

12. If something looks wrong

A new source still says "Not checked yet"
The robot runs all the time, and starts afresh right after a save. Wait a few minutes and press Refresh Feed. The website itself can take a few minutes to show a new save.
A headline is matched to the wrong game
It happens: the robot matches words, so a headline about a different game with a similar name can slip through. Wrong matches drop out by themselves within two weeks as old items age out of the feed. If you see the same wrong match again and again, it's worth reporting.
The number of items has stopped going up
That is how it is meant to work. The feed holds the last two weeks: every run adds what is new and drops what has become too old, so once two weeks are in, the number hovers around the same value. To see whether the robot is still running, look at Last sync in the banner and, with the table's first menu on Everything, at the newest times in the table. The robot keeps itself going: every five and a half hours it hands over to a fresh run. If that ever fails, a backup starts one again, but GitHub can take a few hours to get round to it; Run scraper now restarts it at once.
A game has no eBay median under its name
It isn't priced. Either its name is one ordinary word (Black, Cars, The Sims), or it isn't in the PS2 library, or eBay had no copy that could be counted. In the last case the game is on the eBay prices tab, marked "none listed" or "search needs tuning"; in the first two it isn't on that tab at all.
The eBay prices tab says the price file could not be read
The page asked for the prices and got no proper answer. Press REFRESH; a passing hiccup clears itself, and prices already on screen are kept while it lasts. If the message stays for hours, tell the repository's owner: the file data/prices/latest.json may be damaged.
A game on the eBay prices tab says "search needs tuning"
eBay returned listings, but none of them named the game the way the PS2 library spells it, so no figure is shown. It usually means sellers shorten the title. The owner can fix it by adding the game to ebay_watchlist.json with the words sellers actually use.
A YouTube channel says "Failing: channel link..."
The link didn't lead to a channel. Open it to check. You can also add the channel by its ID: on the channel page open more about this channel → Share channel → Copy channel ID and paste that (it starts with UC).
"Failing: already listed as ..."
That channel is already in the list under another name. Switch the new one off.
A site, forum or podcast says "Failing: HTTP 403" or "HTTP 404"
The site blocks the robot or moved its feed. Switch it off, or add its new feed link. Forums are the usual case for 403: many sit behind a check that turns away GitHub's computers even though the same link opens fine in your browser.
The Thread button on a 4chan row opens a "404 Not Found" page
The thread has ended. 4chan deletes threads once they fall off the board, often within hours. The row itself is still right: the game was named there when the robot looked.
A 4chan board seems to mention very few games
Expected. The robot reads only the post that opens each thread, only counts names spelt exactly and written as names, and sees a busy board like /v/ only as it stands when a run happens, so threads that come and go between runs are never seen.
"feeds.json was changed on GitHub after this page loaded"
Someone else saved in the meantime. Reload the page and make your change again, so theirs isn't lost.
"Unsaved changes" doesn't go away
Finish the save steps and click I've saved it, or press Discard to go back to the saved list.

13. Questions

Why are some of the biggest YouTube channels switched off?
Most are in other languages (the mood score only understands English) or mostly play Minecraft, Roblox or Fortnite, which says little about PS2 games. They are all in the list, so switch any of them on if you want.
Does this page know any prices?
Only eBay asking prices: what sellers want today, shown under each game's name and in full on the eBay prices tab. It knows nothing about what games have sold for. The yellow buttons are searches that open a shop or price guide with the game already looked up.
Why is a game high in Most Mentioned but missing from the Demand Index?
They measure different things. Most Mentioned counts what the sources here are talking about; the Demand Index counts what people are reading about on Wikipedia. A game can be all over the news without anyone looking it up, and the other way round.
Why do 4chan rows say only "Thread on /v/ naming …"?
On purpose. Those boards are tracked for the game names people bring up, and for nothing else, so nothing a poster wrote is stored or shown here: no thread titles, no comments, no names. What you see is a line the robot writes itself. See About the 4chan rows.
Where do the subscriber and rating numbers come from?
